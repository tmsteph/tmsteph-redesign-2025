Technology

Tools that serve life — attention, agency, repairability, open systems, AI, automation, resilience, and the right relationship between humans and machines.

Technology should expand human capability without requiring human captivity.

The starting point

Human beings make tools. Tools extend the hand, eye, memory, voice, imagination, strength, and reach of a community.

The Human Scale Doctrine is not against technological advancement. It is against treating advancement as self-justifying.

The better question is not only “Can we build it?” but “What human purpose does it serve, what does it ask from us, who controls it, and can we leave it?”
Part I — Diagnosis

Technology becomes environment

Some technologies are tools we pick up and put down. Others become the environment through which daily life must pass: phones, payment systems, transportation networks, platforms, workplace software, and AI.

Attention

Digital systems can help us think and connect, but can also compete continuously for the mind.

Dependence

Convenience can quietly trade away competence, repairability, privacy, resilience, or the ability to leave.

Opacity

Necessary complexity is real, but important systems should remain legible enough for people to understand choices, control, and failure.

Scale

Large systems can create extraordinary capability, but concentration can also create lock-in and single points of failure.

Ownership should mean agency

A person may buy a device but still be unable to repair it, modify it, install alternatives, move data, or keep using it when a remote service disappears.

Repair, interoperability, data portability, documented interfaces, replaceable components, local control, and long-lived standards can make ownership more real.

AI changes the relationship between humans and tools

AI can generate, recommend, summarize, search, classify, predict, plan, communicate, and act on our behalf.

The question is whether AI strengthens human judgment and capability — or slowly makes human judgment unnecessary to operate the surrounding world.

Technology can genuinely liberate

A useful critique of technology must preserve its benefits. Tools can reduce drudgery, expand accessibility, connect people, spread knowledge, support medicine, help small teams create, and give ordinary people capabilities once reserved for large institutions.

The goal is not less capability. It is capability without unnecessary domination.

Part II — Field Guide

Use tools without letting them become the whole environment

Judge the aftermath

Ask what happens after months of use: does the tool save time, build competence, support relationships, or create new burdens?

Protect attention

Turn off unnecessary interruptions and preserve spaces where nothing is demanding a click, reaction, or response.

Prefer an exit

Favor standard formats, exports, offline modes, repairable parts, alternative software, and tools that do not trap your work.

Keep competence

When a skill matters for judgment, safety, identity, or resilience, preserve enough human ability to understand what the tool is doing.

Use AI to extend yourself

Let AI explain, draft, automate, organize, and challenge ideas while keeping consequential judgment and direction human.

Let it disappear

Use the map, then look at the street. Use the group chat, then eat together. Use AI, then spend the saved time living.

Part III — Program

Build technological freedom into the system

Repair

Support access to parts, service information, independent repair, reasonable lifetimes, and software support that does not strand useful hardware.

Interoperability

Use open standards, portable data, documented protocols, and competitive clients to lower the cost of leaving.

Calm defaults

Design notifications, recommendations, privacy choices, stopping points, and child-facing systems around human attention rather than maximum engagement.

Public-interest infrastructure

Where digital systems become essential, consider open standards, accountable governance, reusable software, and alternatives to total vendor dependence.

Resilience

Preserve offline modes, backups, local copies, manual overrides, multiple providers, and graceful failure where consequences are serious.

Local capability

Use open source, fabrication, community networks, repair shops, makerspaces, local energy, and shared technical knowledge to distribute capability.

AI should multiply agency

AI is most human-compatible when it helps ordinary people do things that previously demanded large amounts of money, expertise, bureaucracy, or institutional access.

When AI acts on behalf of a person, the person should be able to set boundaries, review consequential actions, correct the system, and revoke authority.

Automation should return something to humans

Automation can create lower prices, higher output, wages, profits, public revenue, better services, shorter work, or some combination. The distribution is not technologically predetermined.

Human Scale asks explicitly: who receives the benefit when a machine removes human labor?

The digital world is still physical

Chips, servers, cables, mines, factories, energy, cooling, shipping, labor, and disposal sit behind digital services. Durability, repair, reuse, materials, energy, and working conditions belong inside technological thinking too.

A Human Scale technology test

What human need or purpose does it serve?
Does it save human time or create new expectations?
Does it strengthen or weaken agency?
What does it do to attention?
Can people understand the important choices and consequences?
Can users repair, export, switch, modify, or leave where appropriate?
Does it preserve meaningful human judgment in consequential decisions?
What happens when the network, company, model, account, or power fails?
Who controls the system and receives the benefits?
Does it strengthen local capability or create unnecessary dependence?
What physical and ecological costs sit behind it?
Does it ultimately leave more room for human life?

A technology does not need a perfect score. The test asks questions that feature lists and adoption numbers often do not.

The deeper idea

Build better medicine. Better energy. Better computers. Robots that take dangerous work. AI that helps people learn, create, organize, and understand systems that once excluded them.

But do not confuse a world full of machines with a world improved for humans.

The most advanced civilization may be one where extraordinary technological capability works quietly underneath healthy bodies, strong communities, living landscapes, meaningful work, free time, creativity, spiritual life, and human relationships.

Use the machine. Receive the benefit. Then return to life.

The best technology expands the human world without replacing it.
