Two-minute introduction

Human Scale

A living attempt to answer one question: how do we keep the extraordinary capabilities of modern civilization while rebuilding everyday life around actual human beings?

Humanity became powerful faster than it became wise.

Industrial energy, machines, markets, institutions, cars, networks, and computers gave us capabilities previous generations could barely imagine. They also allowed us to redesign the human environment faster than we understood what human bodies, minds, relationships, and communities need.

The answer is not to return to the past. The answer is to become more deliberate about what progress is for.

The basic idea

Keep the medicine. Keep the science. Keep the computers. Keep abundant energy, trade, cities, specialization, and large systems where they work.

And recover movement, sleep, nature, time, family, friendship, useful competence, local participation, repairability, public space, living land, understandable institutions, and the ability to leave systems that stop serving us.

Human Scale does not mean small is always better. It means scale has to justify itself.

The intellectual rule

Human Scale distinguishes evidence, hypothesis, value judgment, and political proposal. The project should be allowed to have convictions. It should not be allowed to hide uncertainty.

If reality contradicts the doctrine, the doctrine should change.

The direction

Human Scale is neither anti-modern nor acceleration-at-any-cost.

The village and the computer can coexist.

The work is figuring out how.
