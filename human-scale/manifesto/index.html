Human Scale Manifesto

Modern civilization became extraordinarily capable. The next task is making that capability answerable to human life.

Humanity became powerful faster than it became wise.

We learned to move faster, build larger, extract more, compute more, coordinate more people, and transform environments at unprecedented speed. But our ability to redesign civilization outran our understanding of what kind of civilization human beings actually need.

The answer is not primitivism. It is not technophobia. It is not maximum growth, maximum localism, maximum government, maximum markets, or maximum automation.

The answer is deliberate capability in service of life.

Ten commitments

1
Begin with the human organism.

Bodies, minds, relationships, attention, development, and ecology are constraints civilization cannot wish away.

2
Do not confuse efficiency with flourishing.

More output, speed, convenience, or profit can coexist with worse everyday life.

3
Keep modern capability.

Science, medicine, abundant energy, computing, trade, cities, specialization, and large infrastructure remain extraordinary achievements where they genuinely serve us.

4
Recover the human world.

Movement, sleep, nature, family, friendship, beauty, useful competence, public space, play, meaning, quiet, and human time deserve a place in ordinary life.

5
Make technology a tool again.

Prefer systems that expand capability while preserving attention, ownership, repair, interoperability, judgment, privacy, and exit.

6
Make scale justify itself.

Keep decisions close when that works. Scale up when rights, infrastructure, science, emergencies, ecology, or cross-boundary problems genuinely require it.

7
Return some progress as time.

If technology and productivity save human labor, ask whether people receive some of that gain as freedom rather than only more expected output.

8
Treat the living world as infrastructure.

Healthy soil, water, shade, habitat, gardens, trees, biodiversity, food systems, and access to living land are not decorative extras.

9
Build practical agency.

People should be able to understand, influence, repair, leave, own, create, and participate in more of the systems shaping their lives.

10
Stay corrigible.

Separate evidence, hypotheses, values, and political proposals. State uncertainty. Seek contrary evidence. Change the doctrine when reality defeats it.

What this points toward

Walkable and accessible places. Strong families and chosen families. Public space that does not require a purchase. Gardens and living land close to daily life. Work that leaves room for being alive. Reliable abundant energy. Open and repairable technology where practical. Productive markets with real competition and meaningful ownership. Capable government that remains understandable and accountable. AI that removes drudgery without removing human agency.

Not every system should be small. Not every tradition should be recovered. Not every new technology should be adopted. Not every old technology should be discarded.

The test is the life the system makes possible.

The village and the computer can coexist.

Human Scale is an attempt to figure out how.
