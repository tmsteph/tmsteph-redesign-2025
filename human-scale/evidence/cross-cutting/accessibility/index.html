Whole-doctrine pressure test

Disability & Accessibility

If “human scale” silently means healthy, able-bodied, neurotypical, independently mobile, and easy to accommodate, then the philosophy is scaled to only one subset of humans.

Accessibility is not a concession to exceptional humans. Human variation is part of the scale.

The core correction

WHO frames disability as part of the human experience and as an interaction among health conditions, environmental factors, and personal factors. The Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities treats accessibility across buildings, transportation, information, communications, technology, and public services as a rights issue.

WHO Disability ↗ · WHO Health Equity & Disability ↗ · CRPD Article 9 ↗

Accessibility changes the meaning of agency, independence, technology, place, community, care, work, education, and localism across the whole doctrine.

Independence can require support

CRPD Article 19 explicitly recognizes personal assistance and support services as compatible with independent living and inclusion in the community. Human Scale should therefore define independence as meaningful control over one's life — not the absence of assistance.

CRPD Article 19 ↗

Assistive technology is paradigmatic Human Scale technology

Wheelchairs, hearing aids, prostheses, glasses, captioning, speech recognition, communication systems, memory aids, and other assistive products can make ordinary participation possible. For many people, advanced technology is not what removes them from “natural life.” It is what returns capability.

WHO Assistive Technology ↗ · WHO Rehabilitation ↗

Functioning beats ideological purity. An open, local, minimalist tool is not more humane if the person cannot rely on it.

Across the doctrine

Human Nature

Human variation is part of human nature. Do not define normal functioning by majority performance.

Land & Life

Create a portfolio of access: routes, rest, shade, toilets, sensory information, accessible gardening, and alternatives — without requiring every wild place to serve every body identically.

Community

Digital relationships and remote participation can be essential accessibility infrastructure, not second-best community.

Work & Time

Flexibility, remote work, assistive technology, accessible recruitment, task accommodation, transportation, and recovery time belong inside the good-work test.

Technology

Assistive technology is one of the strongest cases for powerful technology. Privacy, support, reliability, compatibility, cost, and maintainability all matter.

Energy

Reliable power can be medical infrastructure: respiratory devices, refrigeration, elevators, powered mobility, communication, cooling, heating, and charging.

Economics

Equal income can produce unequal real capability when disability adds costs or constrains work and transportation. Benefit cliffs can punish attempts to gain independence.

Governance & Rights

Meetings, voting, hearings, documents, complaints, courts, emergency notices, and appeals must be usable before participation can be called equal.

Food

Swallowing needs, medically necessary diets, sensory differences, dexterity, packaging, kitchen design, delivery, and caregiver burden complicate “cook from scratch” advice.

Architecture & Mobility

Walkability is incomplete without rollability, rest, transit accessibility, parking where needed, alternative channels, and safe evacuation.

Education

Maintain ambitious learning while providing different routes to participation, communication, assessment, and independence.

Health & Family Care

Support should increase autonomy for both the person receiving care and the caregiver. Neither family nor professional systems automatically know best.

Attention & Media

Minimalist interfaces can hide state or cues that some users need. Prefer configurable clarity over one idea of calm.

Spirituality

Do not treat disability as punishment, inspiration spectacle, moral failure, or something that must be spiritually fixed.

AI

AI can expand captioning, description, communication, memory, navigation, and executive-function support — or create inaccessible automated decisions with weak recourse.

Doctrine-wide changes

Human variation by default

Age, disability, neurotype, chronic illness, and temporary impairment belong in the baseline model.

Independence = control

Support can increase independence rather than negate it.

Accessible mobility

Replace walkability-only thinking with realistic multimodal access.

Configurable clarity

Replace one minimalist ideal with user-adjustable information and prompting.

Digital participation

Remote access can be a first-class mode of belonging and civic life.

Design with people

Lived expertise belongs in research, governance, testing, and post-launch feedback.

What would change our minds?

Revise any accessibility intervention when disabled users show that it reduces capability, creates new barriers, increases safety risk, or imposes disproportionate costs compared with better alternatives. Accessibility needs can conflict; configurable and multiple-channel systems may outperform one fictional universal interface.

Result of Cross-Cutting Audit 01

Human Scale should not ask disabled people to become more compatible with a preferred human environment. It should ask how bodies, tools, support, institutions, and environments can be arranged so more people have real capability, participation, dignity, and choice.
