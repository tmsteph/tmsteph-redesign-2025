Cross-Cutting Audits

A chapter can look coherent by itself while still relying on a hidden assumption that fails everywhere else. These audits pressure-test the assumptions running through the whole doctrine.

The next stage is not more compartments. It is finding the assumptions that contaminate several compartments at once.
Cross-Cutting 01Disability & Accessibility →

Human variation belongs in the baseline model. Independence means control, not absence of support; assistive technology and accessible systems can expand agency.

Cross-Cutting 02Aging & End-of-Life →

Age is not capability. Replace aging-in-place ideology with aging with choice of place, supported autonomy, integrated care, and humane end-of-life care.

Cross-Cutting 03Transportation & Mobility →

Replace walkability as master metric with usable access. Modes are tools; safe, reliable participation is the outcome.

Cross-Cutting 04Administrative Burden →

Measure the whole institutional journey. Remove unjustified friction without deleting procedure that protects rights, safety, accuracy, or appeal.

What these audits already change

Human Scale now has to work for supported independence, older bodies, non-drivers, disabled users, caregivers, people navigating institutions under stress, and people whose access depends on technologies or services that a simplistic “small/local/natural” philosophy might dismiss.

Next cross-cutting targets

Ownership & automation distribution; local resilience & disaster response; AI companionship & relational dependence; the Human Scale Audit scoring model; and comparison against competing philosophical and political frameworks.

Human Scale gets more credible each time a hidden default becomes visible.
