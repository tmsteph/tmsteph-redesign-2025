Cross-Cutting Audits

A chapter can look coherent by itself while still relying on a hidden assumption that fails everywhere else. These audits pressure-test the assumptions running through the whole doctrine.

Ten cross-cutting audits now test the defaults that span multiple chapters.
Cross-Cutting 01Disability & Accessibility →

Human variation belongs in the baseline model. Independence means control, not absence of support.

Cross-Cutting 02Aging & End-of-Life →

Age is not capability. Replace aging-in-place ideology with aging with choice of place and supported autonomy.

Cross-Cutting 03Transportation & Mobility →

Replace walkability as a master metric with usable access, safety, reliability, and human time.

Cross-Cutting 04Administrative Burden →

Measure the whole institutional journey and remove unjustified friction without deleting protective procedure.

Cross-Cutting 05Auditing the Human Scale Audit →

The total score is a hypothesis. Test comprehension, overlap, weighting, accessibility, stability, and false precision.

Cross-Cutting 06Resilience & Disaster →

Resilience is not self-sufficiency. Local capacity, regional systems, redundancy, and capable institutions can reinforce one another.

Cross-Cutting 07Ownership & Automation Distribution →

Productivity has no built-in social contract. Separate capability, adoption, output, labor transition, ownership, and distribution.

Cross-Cutting 08AI Companionship & Relational Dependence →

Judge companionship by capability, displacement, dependency, privacy, exit, and the realistic counterfactual.

Cross-Cutting 09Human Scale & Neighboring Frameworks →

Map what Human Scale borrows, where it differs, and what competing traditions expose that it might otherwise miss.

Cross-Cutting 10Implementation Failure →

Founder load, maintenance, adoption, incentives, burden shifts, equity, succession, and stop conditions are part of whether an idea works.

What these audits change

Human Scale now has to work for supported independence, older bodies, non-drivers, disabled users, caregivers, people navigating institutions under stress, people living through disasters, workers facing automation, and people whose access or relationships depend on technologies a simplistic “small/local/natural” philosophy might dismiss.

The next frontier

Cross-cutting coverage is now broad enough that the priority shifts outward: external criticism, Audit calibration, real implementation receipts, comparative measurement, and public experiments that can make the doctrine weaker or more precise.
