Institutional time, friction & rights

Administrative Burden

A form can take ten minutes and still sit inside a process that consumes ten hours, three trips, five phone calls, two logins, a missed work shift, and weeks of uncertainty.

The goal is not zero bureaucracy. It is justified procedure with the lowest human burden compatible with the function.

Measure the whole journey

GAO's 2025 review treats administrative burden as more than time spent typing into a form. Learning whether a person qualifies, figuring out how to apply, assembling documents, complying with requirements, and psychological costs can all matter.

GAO Administrative Burden ↗ · Performance.gov Holistic Burden ↗

The Bureaucracy Receipt

A Human Scale receipt should record learning time, forms, fields, duplicate requests, documents, logins, calls, transfers, hold time, trips, waiting days, lost wages, status uncertainty, accessibility barriers, correction, appeal, and whether another person had to help.

Also record why the step exists. A painful step may protect safety, privacy, accuracy, public money, due process, or appeal. The test is whether the same function can be achieved with less burden.

Burden can become an access barrier

Administrative friction can affect whether people receive food assistance, healthcare, disability support, housing aid, unemployment benefits, disaster assistance, education aid, permits, licensing, legal rights, or other services. People with more time, money, institutional literacy, stable internet, transportation, executive function, or professional help can often navigate the same nominal rule more easily.

Digitization is not automatically simplification

Digital can remove burden

Travel, mailing, handwriting, duplicate entry, scheduling, waiting, and information search can shrink dramatically.

Digital can add burden

Account creation, identity verification, upload failures, inaccessible interfaces, scanner requirements, browser problems, automated dead ends, and loss of human escalation.

Federal Customer Experience ↗ · A-11 CX Guidance ↗

Digitize the function, not the paper. A bad process online is still a bad process.

Status visibility is infrastructure

People should be able to know whether a submission arrived, what stage it is in, what is missing, who owns the case, what timing is normal, and how to correct an error. Uncertainty consumes attention even when no form is being completed.

AI: automate friction before rights-critical judgment

AI can explain eligibility, prefill forms, translate, classify documents, summarize cases, route requests, detect missing information, and answer routine questions. It can also hallucinate policy, hide denial logic, create automation bias, or make human escalation harder.

Use stronger safeguards as the system moves from explanation and routing toward consequential judgment.

System burden, not just citizen burden

A reform can make a public interface easier while shifting huge work onto staff, clinicians, teachers, businesses, or contractors. Count user time, worker time, training, exception handling, error correction, maintenance, audit, fraud investigation, accessibility support, downstream appeal, processing time, and cost.

Doctrine-wide corrections

Full-journey measurement

Learning, compliance, waiting, travel, uncertainty, money, correction, and psychological cost all count.

Protective procedure ≠ waste

Due process, safety, privacy, records, and appeal can justify friction.

Channel pluralism

Good self-service plus human escalation where stakes justify it.

Visible state

Case status should not be a mystery.

AI on friction first

Explanation, translation, routing, search, and prefill before opaque high-stakes judgment.

Publish receipts

Before/after measurements turn simplification into something falsifiable.

What would change our minds?

Preserve burdensome procedures when removing them materially increases fraud, error, safety problems, rights violations, or downstream costs and no lower-burden alternative works. Reverse digital-only reforms that exclude users or eliminate recourse. Reject simplification that merely moves labor off the institution and onto the public.

Result of Cross-Cutting Audit 04

A humane institution should ask people for no more time, attention, money, disclosure, travel, repetition, or uncertainty than the legitimate function of the process requires.
