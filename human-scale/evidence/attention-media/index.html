Attention & Media Under Pressure

Attention is finite. That does not make every engaging product manipulative, every algorithm harmful, or every regulation humane.

The goal is not an unfiltered mind. It is meaningful agency over the filters.

What survives strongly

Documented consumer-protection problem

Dark patterns can impair meaningful choice.

FTC work documents interfaces that obscure terms, disguise advertising, obstruct cancellation, manipulate data disclosure, or otherwise trick and trap consumers.

FTC Dark Patterns ↗

Regulatory documentation

Data-intensive media can create privacy and power problems.

FTC's 2024 study documented extensive collection, sharing, retention, targeting, and limited user control among major services studied.

FTC Behind the Screens ↗

Risk signals + uncertainty

Youth social-media effects are heterogeneous.

The Surgeon General identifies benefits and risks and says the evidence is insufficient to conclude social media is sufficiently safe for youth. Design, content, harassment, sleep, displacement, support, age, and individual vulnerability matter.

HHS Advisory ↗

Strong technical problem

Synthetic media creates a provenance problem.

NIST reviews provenance, labeling, watermarking, detection, testing, and auditing approaches without treating one technique as a complete solution.

NIST Synthetic Content ↗

What becomes more careful

Engagement: not automatically harmful. Measure regret, displacement, privacy, sleep, information quality, and whether users endorse the time afterward.
Feeds: chronology is not inherently humane; algorithmic ranking is not inherently manipulative. User control and transparency matter.
Notifications: interrupt in proportion to actual urgency and user intent.
Advertising: can fund useful services; hidden sponsorship, deceptive claims, exploitative targeting, and excessive surveillance are the stronger concerns.
Calm technology: make it configurable and accessibility-aware rather than minimalist by default.

Safety and expression belong together

UNESCO's platform-governance guidelines pair responses to online harms with freedom of expression, access to information, transparency, due process, and human-rights constraints.

UNESCO Platform Governance ↗

Human Scale should reject both “all moderation is censorship” and “safety permits unlimited opaque control.”

The project must obey its own doctrine

Human Scale's advertising standard now functions as a real implementation test: disclose authorship, no fake grassroots support, no sensitive-vulnerability targeting, no repeated unwanted outreach, distinguish facts from values, correct errors, and leave the audience with something useful.

See the Public Launch →

Recommended correction

Attention remains a design constraint.
Define manipulation narrowly. Deception, obstruction, hidden influence, exploitative targeting, and impaired choice.
Keep youth safeguards with explicit uncertainty.
Protect expression and appeal alongside safety.
Treat provenance as layered infrastructure.
Apply every advertising rule to Human Scale itself.

Result of Audit 14

A humane media environment should help people find valuable information, relationships, and culture without making deception, surveillance, obstruction, hidden influence, or involuntary attention capture the default business model.
