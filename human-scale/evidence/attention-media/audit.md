# Evidence Audit 14 — Attention & Media Under Pressure

**Human Scale Evidence Atlas — August 2026**

> Attention is finite. That does not make every engaging product manipulative, every algorithm harmful, or every regulation humane.

Chapter 14 treats attention as part of a human life and asks who controls the filters through which information arrives.

The core survives. The audit makes the chapter more precise about four distinctions:

- engagement versus value;
- persuasion versus deception;
- personalization versus loss of agency;
- safety governance versus censorship or overreach.

---

# 1. What survives strongly

## Dark patterns can impair meaningful choice

**Grade: Strong documented consumer-protection problem**

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission has documented interface practices that obscure terms, disguise advertising, make cancellation difficult, steer data disclosure, create misleading scarcity, or otherwise trick and trap consumers.

Human Scale is on firm ground when it treats interface design as capable of changing the quality of consent.

Sources:
- FTC Bringing Dark Patterns to Light: https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light
- FTC overview: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/09/ftc-report-shows-rise-sophisticated-dark-patterns-designed-trick-trap-consumers

## Data-intensive media business models can create privacy and power problems

**Grade: Strong regulatory documentation, mechanisms vary by service**

FTC's 2024 study of major social-media and video-streaming services documented extensive data collection, sharing, retention, ad targeting, and limited user control in the companies studied.

Human Scale should therefore treat privacy and data minimization as part of attention architecture, not as a separate technical issue.

Source:
- FTC A Look Behind the Screens: https://www.ftc.gov/reports/look-behind-screens-examining-data-practices-social-media-video-streaming-services

## Youth social-media effects are heterogeneous and not fully resolved

**Grade: Meaningful risk signals + major uncertainty**

The U.S. Surgeon General's advisory identifies both benefits and risks and explicitly states that the evidence is insufficient to conclude social media is sufficiently safe for children and adolescents.

The correct Human Scale response is neither “social media destroys children” nor “the evidence is uncertain so do nothing.”

Age, content, time, design, harassment, social comparison, displacement, sleep, support, identity, and individual vulnerability all matter.

Source:
- HHS Surgeon General, Social Media and Youth Mental Health: https://www.hhs.gov/surgeongeneral/reports-and-publications/youth-mental-health/social-media/index.html

## Synthetic media creates a real provenance problem

**Grade: Strong technical problem / imperfect solution space**

NIST has documented technical approaches to synthetic-content transparency including provenance, labeling, watermarking, detection, testing, and auditing.

Human Scale should support better provenance without pretending any one detection or watermarking technique solves misinformation.

Source:
- NIST, Reducing Risks Posed by Synthetic Content: https://www.nist.gov/publications/reducing-risks-posed-synthetic-content-overview-technical-approaches-digital-content

# 2. What becomes more careful

## “Engagement optimization is bad”

**Grade: Reject as blanket claim**

Engagement can signal usefulness: a good book, lesson, conversation, game, tool, or community can deserve sustained attention.

The problem arises when a system optimizes measurable engagement while externalizing costs such as sleep loss, deception, compulsive use, harassment, privacy loss, distorted information quality, or regret.

A stronger test is:

**Do users still endorse the time and behavior after the fact, with realistic knowledge of how the system works?**

## “Chronological feeds are more humane”

**Grade: Design option, not settled science**

Chronology increases user predictability but can overwhelm people with volume, reward frequent posters, and bury relevant information.

Algorithmic ranking can reduce overload and increase relevance while also concentrating invisible editorial power.

Human Scale should support meaningful controls and transparency rather than canonize one feed architecture.

## “Notifications are harmful”

**Grade: Context dependent**

Alerts can interrupt, but they can also communicate emergencies, accessibility events, medical reminders, direct messages, safety information, and time-sensitive work.

The stronger design principle is:

**interrupt in proportion to actual urgency and user intent.**

## “Advertising corrupts media”

**Grade: Business-model risk, not universal rule**

Advertising can fund free journalism, entertainment, search, maps, communication, and public information.

It becomes more problematic when sponsorship is hidden, targeting exploits sensitive vulnerabilities, data collection is excessive, claims are deceptive, or the platform's incentives strongly reward attention capture over user welfare.

Human Scale's own advertising therefore has to obey the standards it asks of others.

---

# 3. Freedom of expression is part of media safety

UNESCO's platform-governance guidelines explicitly pair responses to online harms with freedom of expression, access to information, due process, transparency, and human-rights constraints.

Source:
- UNESCO Guidelines for the Governance of Digital Platforms: https://www.unesco.org/en/internet-trust/guidelines

Human Scale should reject two symmetric errors:

1. treating all content moderation or platform governance as censorship;
2. treating “safety” as permission for opaque or unlimited control of lawful expression.

A humane governance model should ask:

- what rule exists;
- who makes it;
- what evidence supports it;
- how it is enforced;
- whether users receive notice;
- whether there is appeal;
- whether enforcement is consistent;
- whether government pressure is visible and rights-constrained;
- whether children and high-risk settings justify different safeguards;
- whether users can choose among filters or services.

---

# 4. Attention is also an accessibility question

“Quiet technology” should not mean removing information some users need.

People with ADHD, autism, sensory differences, memory impairment, visual/hearing disability, anxiety, caregiving demands, or complex work may benefit from very different levels of prompting and filtering.

Human Scale should therefore prefer **configurable calm** over one minimalist ideal.

A good interface may be quiet for one user and explicit, repetitive, multimodal, or highly structured for another.

---

# 5. Political communication deserves extra care

Political speech is central to democracy and uniquely vulnerable to manipulation concerns.

Human Scale's own political/civic communication should therefore:

- identify authorship and sponsorship;
- avoid fake grassroots support;
- avoid fabricated social proof;
- avoid sensitive-vulnerability targeting;
- distinguish empirical claims from values;
- correct factual errors visibly;
- avoid harassment and repeated unwanted outreach;
- link source material where feasible;
- invite criticism without using criticism as engagement bait.

The project's advertising ethics are therefore not an accessory. They are an implementation test of Chapter 14.

---

# 6. Recommended Chapter 14 changes

1. **Keep attention as a finite-life resource and design constraint.**
2. **Define manipulation more narrowly around deception, obstruction, hidden influence, exploitative targeting, and impaired choice.**
3. **Do not equate engagement with harm.** Measure regret, displacement, privacy, sleep, outcomes, user intent, and information quality.
4. **Do not canonize chronological feeds.** Support meaningful ranking controls and transparency.
5. **Treat notifications by urgency and user intent.**
6. **Keep youth safeguards while stating uncertainty and heterogeneity.**
7. **Protect freedom of expression and due process alongside safety.**
8. **Treat provenance as layered infrastructure, not a magic watermark.**
9. **Make calm technology configurable and accessibility-aware.**
10. **Apply the doctrine's advertising standard to Human Scale itself.**

---

# 7. What would change our minds?

Human Scale should weaken claims about engagement-driven harms where strong causal evidence shows little harm after user selection and underlying vulnerability are addressed.

It should strengthen specific design restrictions where randomized, quasi-experimental, or real-world policy evaluations show improved well-being, privacy, sleep, informed choice, or youth safety without disproportionate costs to expression or access.

It should reject provenance technologies that create false confidence, are easily stripped or forged, or impose larger privacy/censorship risks than the problem they solve.

It should revise its own launch and advertising if real users experience the campaign as spammy, manipulative, over-targeted, or more certain than the underlying evidence.

---

# Result of Audit 14

**A humane media environment should help people find valuable information, relationships, and culture without making deception, surveillance, obstruction, hidden influence, or involuntary attention capture the default business model.**

The goal is not an unfiltered mind.

The goal is meaningful agency over the filters.
