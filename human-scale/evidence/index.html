Evidence & uncertainty

Human Scale Evidence Atlas

What do we actually know, what do we merely suspect, what do we value, and what are we choosing politically?

A philosophy becomes more trustworthy when it knows which parts are evidence, which parts are hypotheses, and which parts are choices.

Why this exists

The doctrine makes claims about bodies, relationships, land, work, technology, place, childhood, health, ecology, institutions, and civilization. Those claims should not all be treated as though they have the same kind of support.

The purpose is not to decorate the doctrine with citations. The purpose is to make it harder for the doctrine to lie to itself.

The labels

Strong evidence

Consistent empirical support

Substantial evidence supports the claim as stated, while details may still be uncertain.

Moderate

Direction is credible

Causality, magnitude, population, dose, or implementation remains meaningfully uncertain.

Hypothesis

Worth testing, not declaring

A plausible principle that must stay vulnerable to failure.

Value / proposal

A choice, not a discovery

Evidence can inform it, but science alone cannot decide what society ought to value.

Claims we should not call settled science

Working hypothesis

Human-scale systems preserve agency and resilience.

Local systems can also be under-resourced, exclusionary, corrupt or incapable of solving cross-boundary problems.

Value + hypothesis

Open, repairable technology protects agency.

Exit and ownership matter, but security, coordination, support, incentives and complexity create tradeoffs.

Value + proposal

Productivity gains should return some human time.

Evidence can measure the gains. It cannot decide their just distribution.

Value + proposal

Meaningful living-world access should be broadly available.

Evidence informs this commitment; Human Scale chooses it as a political value.

Political design

Power should operate at the smallest effective scale.

Subsidiarity is a design principle, not a law of nature.

Design value

Advanced technology should become quieter.

Test it through time saved, interruptions, skill, privacy, exit and outcomes.

What would change our minds?

Local systems repeatedly perform worse without meaningful gains in agency, resilience or accountability.
Supposed mismatch effects disappear under stronger causal designs.
Specific green, garden or nature interventions fail to produce the intended benefits after confounders are addressed.
Human Scale land projects create persistent cost, safety, water, maintenance or displacement burdens.
Shorter-work experiments consistently worsen well-being or material security once income and workload are controlled.
Open systems create harms that cannot be mitigated without destroying the intended benefit.
Modern high-tech environments meet human needs as well as or better than lower-tech alternatives without hidden burdens.
A favored policy creates large unintended burdens for the people it was supposed to help.
A doctrine that cannot lose an argument is not learning.
