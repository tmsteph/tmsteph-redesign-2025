Evidence & uncertainty

Human Scale Evidence Atlas

What do we actually know, what do we merely suspect, what do we value, and what are we choosing politically?

The audit counts only if it is allowed to make our favorite idea weaker.

The method

Human Scale is not one empirical claim that can be proven or falsified by a single paper. It is a worldview containing hundreds of smaller claims. The Atlas breaks those claims apart so evidence can challenge them individually.

Strong evidence

Consistent support

Support is substantial for the claim as narrowly stated; details and implementation can still be uncertain.

Moderate

Credible direction

Causality, magnitude, population, dose, or transfer to policy remains meaningfully uncertain.

Hypothesis

Worth testing

A plausible mechanism that must remain vulnerable to domain-specific failure.

Value / proposal

A choice

Evidence can constrain and inform it. Science alone cannot decide what a civilization ought to value.

Research rule: separate evidence → inference → values → political mechanism. Then ask what result would make us change our minds.

Deep audits

Eleven chapters have now been put under explicit pressure. Several audits have already narrowed or corrected the public doctrine.

01 · Human Nature →Mismatch survives; novelty is not evidence of harm and ancestral conditions are not automatic prescriptions. 02 · Land & Life →Ecological dependence survives; gardening, food forests, and “reconnection” become site-specific interventions. 03 · Community →Supportive connection survives; localism becomes one mechanism rather than the definition of belonging. 04 · Work & Time →Human time remains central; reduced-hours designs become experiments rather than one universal schedule. 05 · Technology →Agency survives; open, local, repairable, interoperable, and quiet are tools whose tradeoffs must be tested. 06 · Energy →Abundant reliable power survives; strong grids and distributed resilience become complements rather than ideological alternatives. 07 · Economics →Practical freedom remains the goal; ownership, markets, security, competition, and redistribution remain mechanisms to compare. 08 · Governance →Subsidiarity survives as a testable design principle bounded by rights, state capacity, coordination, and accountability. 09 · Food →Safety, nourishment, affordability, resilience, and ecological durability outrank local, natural, or homemade identity labels. 10 · Architecture & Place →Human Scale architecture becomes functional, not stylistic: access, safety, heat, time, housing, accessibility, ecology, and real use. 11 · Education & Childhood →Knowledge, movement, sleep, belonging, capability, and plural pathways survive; AI must scaffold rather than substitute for capability.

Corrections that now define the project

Novel does not mean harmful.

Evolutionary mismatch is a question to investigate, not an answer to assume.

Small does not mean good.

Scale follows the function. Small systems can fail; large systems can be necessary and humane.

Local does not mean sustainable.

Food, energy, community, and production have to be compared by outcomes and resilience, not distance alone.

Open does not mean secure or usable.

Exit, repair, interoperability, safety, support, complexity, and maintainability all count.

Free time is not enough.

Material security, contribution, predictable control, health, and meaningful choice matter alongside hours.

One human ideal is not human-scale.

Disability, age, temperament, culture, family form, geography, and life stage change what supportive environments look like.

What would make Human Scale lose?

Supposed mismatch effects disappear under stronger causal designs.
Smaller or local systems repeatedly perform worse without meaningful gains in agency, resilience, rights, or accountability.
Favored interventions create persistent cost, safety, maintenance, exclusion, or ecological burdens larger than their benefits.
Open or interoperable systems produce harms that cannot be mitigated without destroying the intended advantage.
High-tech environments meet human needs as well as or better than quieter alternatives without hidden burdens.
People with meaningful choices consistently prefer arrangements the doctrine expects to be worse while achieving equal or better outcomes.
A policy aimed at vulnerable people predictably makes their real choices narrower.
Better evidence contradicts an evidence grade published here.
A doctrine that cannot lose an argument is not learning.
