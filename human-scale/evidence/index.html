Evidence & uncertainty

Human Scale Evidence Atlas

What do we actually know, what do we merely suspect, what do we value, and what are we choosing politically?

A philosophy becomes more trustworthy when it knows which parts are evidence, which parts are hypotheses, and which parts are choices.

Why this exists

The doctrine makes claims about bodies, relationships, work, technology, place, childhood, ecology, institutions, and civilization. Those claims should not all be treated as though they have the same kind of support.

The purpose is not to decorate the doctrine with citations. The purpose is to make it harder for the doctrine to lie to itself.

The labels

Strong evidence

Consistent empirical support

Substantial evidence supports the claim as stated, while details may still be uncertain.

Moderate

Direction is credible

Causality, magnitude, population, dose, or implementation remains meaningfully uncertain.

Hypothesis

Worth testing, not declaring

A plausible principle that must stay vulnerable to failure.

Value / proposal

A choice, not a discovery

Evidence can inform it, but science alone cannot decide what society ought to value.

Deep audits

Broad grades are useful. The real work is taking a chapter apart claim by claim and allowing the public doctrine to change.

Initial claim register

Strong evidence

Regular physical activity matters for health.

Supports designing environments where movement is easier. It does not establish one universal exercise system.

Supportive social connection matters for health and well-being.

The optimal mix of local, face-to-face, family, friendship, community, and digital connection varies.

Chronically very long working hours can create measurable health risk.

Supports treating human time as an occupational-health question. It does not prove one ideal workweek.

Claims we should not call settled science

Working hypothesis

Human-scale systems preserve agency and resilience.

Sometimes. Local systems can also be under-resourced, exclusionary, corrupt, fragile, or incapable of solving cross-boundary problems.

Value + hypothesis

Open, repairable technology protects agency.

Exit and ownership matter, but security, coordination, support, incentives, and complexity create tradeoffs.

Value + proposal

Productivity gains should return some human time.

Evidence can measure productivity and outcomes. It cannot decide how gains ought to be distributed.

Value + proposal

People should have meaningful access to the living world.

This is an ethical and political commitment informed by evidence, not mechanically produced by it.

Political design

Power should operate at the smallest effective scale.

Subsidiarity is a design principle, not a law of nature.

Design value

Advanced technology should become quieter.

This becomes empirical when we test whether tools save time, reduce interruptions, preserve skill, protect exit, and improve outcomes.

What would change our minds?

Local systems repeatedly perform worse without meaningful gains in agency, resilience, or accountability.
Stronger causal studies fail to reproduce expected benefits from specific nature or built-environment interventions.
Shorter-work experiments consistently worsen well-being or material security once income and workload are controlled.
Open or interoperable systems create harms that cannot be mitigated without destroying their intended benefit.
Dense advanced environments satisfy movement, nature, community, childhood, quiet, and agency as well as or better than alternatives.
A favored policy creates large unintended burdens for the people it was supposed to help.
A doctrine that cannot lose an argument is not learning.
