Human Scale Field Guide

A practical guide for living more humanly inside the world that exists right now — without waiting for civilization to be redesigned first.

Start by making ordinary life more compatible with being human.

What this is

The doctrine asks what kind of civilization human beings need. This guide asks a more immediate question: what can I do now?

It is not a purity test, productivity system, anti-modern lifestyle, or command to move to the country.

The aim is not to optimize every hour. It is to make a good life easier to inhabit.

Start with the environment

When a good behavior repeatedly feels difficult, do not ask only what is wrong with your discipline. Ask what the environment makes easy.

Nearby

What is physically easy to reach — food, nature, people, movement, tools, screens?

Time

What does the schedule actually leave room for after work, commuting, care, and administration?

Attention

Which devices and systems have automatic access to your awareness?

Defaults

What small environmental change would make the desired action easier tomorrow?

Willpower matters. Design matters too. Change the default where you can.

The daily anchors

Body

Walk, stretch, carry, play, exercise, practice yoga, work with your hands. Let movement exist outside the formal workout too.

Outside

Spend some time under the real sky. Notice daylight, weather, trees, birds, water, soil, and the place where you actually live.

Food

Eat some food that still resembles ingredients. Cook something when you can. Share a meal when possible.

Living things

Water a plant, tend a garden, care for an animal, sit under a tree, compost, or learn something growing nearby.

People

Have at least one interaction that is more than a transaction: talk, help, listen, play, eat, make, or ask for help.

Attention

Create part of the day where no machine is continuously trying to redirect you.

Inner life

Leave room for prayer, meditation, yoga, music, journaling, contemplation, gratitude, philosophy, or presence.

Ending

Let the day have an ending. Do not let work, messages, news, and entertainment expand until sleep is the only boundary left.

Make the home support the life

Keep useful food, movement, books, instruments, tools, plants, repair supplies, and places to sit together easier to reach than the things you are trying to use less.

Create somewhere devices can become quiet. Keep a few reliable meals and basic emergency supplies. Give prayer, reading, stretching, art, or silence a physical place if you can.

Do not wait for the ideal house. A windowsill, front step, courtyard, library, park, garden, and neighborhood can become part of the home environment.
Land & food

Participate in living systems

Grow one thing

An herb, balcony pot, garden plot, fruit tree, or food forest changes nature from scenery into relationship.

Find shared land

Use parks, community gardens, orchards, trails, school gardens, habitat projects, or public land repeatedly.

Build food competence

Learn a handful of flexible meals, basic pantry foods, storage, leftovers, and simple ingredients without turning food into a moral performance.

Know a producer

When practical, connect with a farmers market, farm, CSA, grower, nursery, or independent food business.

A good food practice should make life more livable, not more anxious.
Work, technology & money

Recover practical agency

Protect human time

Count commuting, scheduling, unpaid messages, recovery, and administration as real costs of work. Protect a real ending where possible.

Keep meaningful work

Preserve at least one useful or creative activity that is not measured by an employer.

Quiet technology

Disable unnecessary notifications, batch messages, make device-free situations, and remove low-value digital demands.

Preserve exit

Prefer repairable tools, portable data, open formats, offline copies, interoperable systems, and technology you can leave.

Use AI deliberately

Let AI reduce bureaucracy and expand capability without outsourcing every judgment, relationship, creative impulse, or difficult thought.

Build margin

Where possible, understand recurring costs, keep reserves, build useful skills, reduce fragile dependence, and gradually own useful or productive things.

Use the machine. Receive the benefit. Return to life.
Community & place

Become part of somewhere

Show up repeatedly

Use the same park, library, market, garden, congregation, class, club, cafe, or public place often enough to become familiar.

Become useful

Offer a meal, ride, tool, skill, introduction, help, attention, or care — and allow others to help you too.

Walk the radius

Notice shade, crossings, gathering places, food, play, trees, gardens, benches, and trips made difficult only by design.

Improve one place

A planter, bench, cleanup, safer route, shared table, garden, tree, library visit, or recurring gathering can matter.

Community usually grows through repetition before it grows through organization.

A simple weekly rhythm

Daily

Movement, outside time, nourishing food, direct human contact, living systems, quieter attention, and an ending to the day.

Weekly

A shared meal, nature or gardening, longer movement or physical work, maintenance, community, and time not organized around buying, working, or scrolling.

Monthly

Review recurring costs, repair or remove something, check household resilience, audit technology, and participate in one local issue when attention is needed.

This is a menu, not a scorecard.

The minimum viable Human Scale day

Some days are chaotic. Make the standard smaller.

Step outside.
Move your body.
Eat one decent meal.
Speak with someone you care about.
Interact with a plant, animal, tree, garden, or other living thing.
Spend a few quiet minutes without incoming information.
Do one useful thing with your hands.
Let the day end.
You do not need to win the day. You need to remain inside your life.

The seven-day reset

1

Observe

Notice the three largest recurring drains on time, attention, money, movement, or emotional energy.

2

Body

Walk, stretch, exercise, work with your hands, go outside, and notice how the environment shapes movement.

3

Food & life

Cook something simple, eat with someone, and care for something living.

4

Technology

Turn off low-value notifications, remove one digital demand, and use a tool intentionally for something useful.

5

Place

Walk the neighborhood and identify one place worth protecting and one thing that could make it more human.

6

People

Call, visit, help, invite, join, or show up. Strengthen one relationship rather than consuming more information about relationships.

7

Design the next week

Keep only one daily anchor, one weekly relationship or nature practice, and one environmental change that makes the desired behavior easier.

When personal friction is a public problem

If many people are individually compensating for the same bad environment, the environment may be what needs to change.

1. Who actually controls this?
2. At what scale can it be changed?
3. Who else experiences the same problem?
4. What is the smallest concrete improvement worth testing?
5. How would we know whether it worked?

Dangerous crossings, inaccessible gardens, bad schedules, locked devices, poor transit, missing shade, confusing bureaucracy, and lack of public gathering places are not always individual failures.

Personal adaptation is useful. It should not become an excuse for permanent institutional failure.

Do not make this another optimization religion

Life includes illness, screens, deadlines, convenience food, long drives, bad weeks, dependence, complexity, and things we cannot control.

Use the hospital. Use the grid. Use the car. Use the computer. Use AI. Buy convenience when you need it. Accept help.

Then keep asking whether these systems are helping you return to life or steadily replacing it.

A good life is not another project-management system.

The direction

Move your body. Go outside. Grow something. Cook something. Know somebody. Make something. Repair something. Protect some time. Quiet the machine sometimes. Pay attention to what is alive. Build enough security to have choices. Improve one place you inhabit. Join with other people when the problem is larger than you. Leave room for mystery.

Human Scale begins wherever a human being stops treating life as an abstraction and starts participating in it again.
