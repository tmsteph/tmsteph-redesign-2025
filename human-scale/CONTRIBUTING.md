# Contributing to Human Scale

Human Scale is a living doctrine, field guide, civic program, evidence atlas, and public lab.

The project gets better when people bring information that changes what it believes or builds.

Useful contribution types include:

- evidence challenges;
- doctrine critiques;
- local experiment reports;
- accessibility critiques;
- failed interventions;
- stronger primary sources;
- design and code improvements;
- clearer explanations;
- implementation receipts;
- counterexamples where large, centralized, proprietary, urban, modern, or otherwise non-Human-Scale-looking systems actually perform better.

## Evidence standard

For empirical claims, prefer:

1. primary research;
2. official data or guidance;
3. systematic reviews or meta-analyses;
4. strong quasi-experimental designs;
5. direct implementation data with enough context to interpret it.

Separate:

- evidence;
- hypothesis;
- value judgment;
- political proposal.

Do not use a scientific citation to disguise a value judgment.

Do not use a value judgment to invent a scientific conclusion.

## Critique standard

A strong critique names:

- the exact claim;
- the population or context where it fails;
- the ignored tradeoff or mechanism;
- a better formulation;
- what should remain from the original idea;
- what would change the critic's mind too.

## Experiment standard

A useful experiment reports:

- the initial problem;
- context;
- intervention;
- expected result;
- cost and human time;
- permissions or institutional dependencies;
- participation and access;
- outcomes;
- failure;
- maintenance;
- unintended consequences;
- what Human Scale should learn.

Do not run unsafe medical, psychological, environmental, engineering, or human-subject experiments. Use professional, ethical, and regulatory safeguards where appropriate.

## Political pluralism

Human Scale is not a party platform.

Contributors may disagree about markets, government, religion, cities, family, energy, schooling, healthcare financing, AI, ownership, land use, and other contested questions.

The project should evaluate mechanisms rather than require ideological identity.

Basic boundaries:

- protect rights and dignity;
- do not harass or dox people;
- do not manufacture engagement;
- do not target people based on sensitive traits;
- do not publish private personal information without permission;
- criticize claims and institutions without turning disagreement into campaigns against individuals.

## What happens to contributions?

A contribution may:

- strengthen a claim;
- narrow a claim;
- reclassify evidence as hypothesis or value;
- change the Field Guide;
- change the Program;
- create a new experiment;
- revise a chapter;
- retire an idea;
- remain an unresolved disagreement.

Human Scale should not merge every contribution into consensus.

It should make important disagreements legible.

**A doctrine that cannot lose an argument is not learning.**
