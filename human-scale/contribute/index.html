What makes a strong contribution?

Be specific. Name the exact claim, page, policy, experiment, or design.
Bring evidence when the claim is empirical. Primary sources, data, official documents, strong reviews, or direct implementation receipts beat argument by vibe.
Separate evidence from values. “This does not cause X” and “I do not value X” are different objections.
Show tradeoffs. A proposal that improves one Human Scale value may worsen another.
Include context. Climate, disability, income, housing, culture, age, governance, geography, and institutional capacity can change what works.
Report failure. Failed pilots are first-class contributions if they prevent repetition.
Say what would change your mind too. Critique should be vulnerable to evidence just like the doctrine.

Contribution boundaries

No harassment, doxxing, manufactured engagement, unsafe experimentation, or political manipulation. Do not publish private health, family, workplace, or identifying information without permission. Do not use the project to target people based on sensitive traits. Critique ideas and systems without turning disagreement into a campaign against individuals.

Human Scale should be pluralistic enough to include people who disagree strongly about markets, government, religion, cities, technology, family structure, energy, and culture—provided the disagreement can remain evidence-aware, rights-respecting, and open to revision.
