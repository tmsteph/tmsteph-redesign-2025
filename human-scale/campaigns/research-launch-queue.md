# Human Scale — Research Launch Queue

**August 2026**

This campaign turns the project's strongest corrections into public artifacts.

Rule:

> **Do not advertise “Human Scale” as the interesting thing. Advertise a useful question, tool, correction, or local result. Let the project name be where the work lives.**

Every launch artifact should do at least one of:

- help someone inspect their own life or environment;
- show a belief becoming more precise after criticism;
- offer a falsifiable question;
- produce a reusable method;
- invite a concrete counterexample;
- create a local public-good artifact.

No coordinated voting. No fake grassroots support. No mass unsolicited DMs. No repeated follow-up to silence. No sensitive-vulnerability targeting. No claim made more certain for marketing than it is in the Evidence Atlas.

# 01 — “I wrote a philosophy, then made it argue with itself”

## Hook

> I wrote a philosophy about making modern civilization work better for actual humans. Then I started publishing evidence audits designed to make my own favorite claims lose.

## Payload

Show 5–8 doctrine corrections:

- novelty ≠ harmful;
- small ≠ good;
- local ≠ sustainable;
- open ≠ automatically free;
- belonging ≠ captivity;
- age ≠ capability;
- walkability ≠ access;
- AI exposure ≠ job loss.

## CTA

Find the next claim that should change.

## Destination

Evidence Atlas / GitHub Issue #143.

## Best channels

Hacker News, LinkedIn, Bluesky, philosophy/technology communities where self-promotion is allowed.

# 02 — “Would you delete your own score?”

## Hook

> I built a five-minute Human Scale Audit — and now I’m testing whether the total score is the worst part of it.

## Payload

Explain:

- two very different lives can add to the same number;
- weights hide value judgments;
- a commute can be counted several times through sleep/time/community/movement;
- disability or caregiving must not be graded as personal failure;

## CTA

Take the Audit and tell us exactly where one question or the total score breaks.

## Destination

Audit + GitHub Issue #145.

## Best channels

UX, survey methods, quantified-self, indie hackers, accessibility, data visualization, behavioral science communities.

# 03 — “Independence does not mean no support”

## Hook

> One of the biggest corrections to Human Scale came from disability: independence is control over your life, not the absence of support.

## Payload

Examples:

- wheelchair;
- screen reader;
- personal care attendant;
- medication;
- paratransit;
- accessible housing;
- remote work;
- AI executive-function assistance.

## CTA

## Destination

Cross-Cutting Disability & Accessibility Audit.

## Best channels

Accessibility, civic tech, product design, urban design, disability communities — only where the post is invited/relevant and authorship is disclosed.

# 04 — “Aging in place is not the goal”

## Hook

> “Aging in place” sounds humane until the place becomes stairs, isolation, driving dependence, caregiver exhaustion, or no nearby care.

## Payload

Replacement principle:

> **Aging with choice of place.**

Keep adapted homes, multigenerational living, accessible apartments, supportive housing, assisted living, skilled nursing, memory care, and relocation available as tools.

## CTA

Ask older adults and caregivers what would make their current place stop working.

## Destination

Aging & End-of-Life audit.

# 05 — “Walkability is not access”

## Hook

> I like walkable cities. But “walkability” is a bad master metric for a philosophy that claims to include people who can’t walk far — or at all.

## Payload

Replace with:

> **usable access**

Measure:

- door-to-door time;
- safety;
- cost;
- reliability;
- walking/wheeling/cycling;
- transit;
- car access;
- delivery;
- remote access;
- disability and age;
- fallback when one mode fails.

## CTA

Ask people for one trip in their city that looks “close” on a map but is functionally far.

## Destination

Transportation & Mobility audit / San Diego receipt.

# 06 — “Give bureaucracy a receipt”

## Hook

> A government form can take 10 minutes and still sit inside a process that consumes 10 hours, 3 trips, 5 phone calls, a missed shift, and weeks of uncertainty.

## Payload

Introduce the **Bureaucracy Receipt**:

- learning time;
- logins;
- forms;
- duplicate fields;
- documents;
- calls/transfers/hold;
- travel;
- active time;
- elapsed days;
- status visibility;
- accessibility;
- appeal;
- lost work/care time;

Important:

> Not every step is waste. Some protect rights, safety, accuracy, privacy, or public money.

## CTA

Nominate one low-risk San Diego process to measure.

## Destination

Issue #144 / Lab.

# 07 — “Resilience is not off-grid”

## Hook

> A resilient neighborhood is not one that no longer needs the grid, hospitals, water treatment, emergency response, or regional logistics.

## Payload

Replacement:

> **nested resilience**

Household + community + city/county + regional + state/national systems should overlap enough that one failure is not total dependency.

Backup has cost. Local capability and large-system capacity are complements.

## CTA

Ask: which essential service in your area has only one realistic path when something breaks?

## Destination

Resilience & Disaster audit.

# 08 — “AI has no built-in social contract”

## Hook

> AI can make a task take half the time. Nothing in the model decides whether that saved time becomes lower prices, profit, higher wages, more output, fewer jobs, public revenue, ownership returns, or shorter hours.

## Payload

Separate:

- capability;
- adoption;
- productivity;
- labor transition;
- distribution.

Then ask:

> Who receives the productivity gain — and who pays for the transition?

## CTA

Invite economists, founders, workers, labor researchers, and policy people to attack the distribution framework.

## Destination

Ownership & Automation Distribution audit.

# 09 — “What does an AI companion displace?”

## Hook

> “AI companions replace human relationships” is too simple. An hour with AI might replace a friend — or doomscrolling, rumination, journaling, gaming, sleep, therapy prep, or being alone.

## Payload

Ask:

- What was the intended use?
- What would the person likely have done instead?
- Does it bridge toward wanted human connection or avoid it?
- Can the person stop?
- What business model benefits from attachment?
- What happens to intimate data?

## CTA

Invite people who actually use conversational AI for companionship to describe benefits and costs without demanding that they defend whether the AI is “real.”

## Destination

AI Companionship audit.

# Launch-order recommendation

Do **not** publish all of these at once.

A good organic sequence is:

1. Audit score critique;
2. philosophy-corrected-itself / Evidence Atlas;
3. one accessibility correction;
4. one San Diego receipt request;
5. one AI/economics correction;
6. implementation failure;
7. neighboring frameworks.

Use later posts only when earlier conversations reveal real interest or new evidence.

## Stop condition

If a channel produces clicks but no substantive replies, Audit use, evidence criticism, experiment participation, or return visits after several tries, stop treating that channel as validated distribution.

Do not compensate by posting more often.

Change the artifact or the audience hypothesis.

# What counts as an advertising win

Priority order:

1. outside criticism that changes a claim;
2. someone else runs a Lab experiment;
3. a San Diego receipt gets completed;
4. an outside contributor improves evidence/code/design;
5. repeat readers return to Evidence/Lab;
6. Audit completions that generate useful feedback;
7. high-quality discussion;
8. only then: raw reach, likes, followers, pageviews.

**The growth loop should reward the project for becoming more useful, not more inflammatory.**
