# Human Scale San Diego — Pilot Pack v0.1

**August 2026**

> Start smaller than a revolution. Make one place easier to live in, document what happened, and learn in public.

San Diego is the first natural place to test Human Scale in the physical world.

The objective is not to create a political organization before we have evidence that people want one.

The first objective is to produce **useful local receipts**:

- what problem exists;
- who controls it;
- what one small change would look like;
- how much time and money it takes;
- who participates;
- who is excluded;
- what improves;
- what fails.

Then share the result.

This makes promotion part of the work rather than a separate marketing performance.

# Pilot 1 — Third-Place & Human-Time Map

## Question

Where can a person spend time around other people without needing to spend much money, drive far, or join a private institution?

## What to map

In one neighborhood or transit corridor, record:

- library branches;
- parks;
- plazas;
- recreation centers;
- playgrounds;
- community rooms;
- beaches or trails;
- community gardens;
- inexpensive cafes;
- public markets;
- faith institutions with public/community uses;
- benches and shaded gathering areas.

For each place record:

- cost;
- hours;
- bathroom access;
- shade/shelter;
- transit;
- walking conditions;
- wheelchair/stroller access;
- child friendliness;
- whether people can linger;
- repeat use;
- whether a person can be present without purchasing something.

## First output

A one-page neighborhood social-infrastructure map and a list of the three most obvious gaps.

## Why it advertises the project

People can argue about “community” abstractly forever.

A useful local map is shareable because it answers a real question.

Invite residents to add missing places or challenge the criteria.

# Pilot 2 — Shade, Walking & Public-Space Receipt

## Question

What is one short route that is unnecessarily difficult to walk because of heat, crossing safety, missing shade, sidewalk problems, or poor public-space design?

## Method

Choose one ordinary trip:

- home to trolley/bus;
- library to transit;
- school to park;
- apartment area to groceries;
- senior housing to daily services;
- workplace to transit.

Record:

- route length;
- shade;
- crossings;
- traffic speed/comfort;
- sidewalk condition;
- accessibility barriers;
- seating;
- drinking water/restrooms where relevant;
- heat exposure;
- avoidable detours.

Use the City of San Diego’s **Get It Done** system for reportable maintenance/service problems where appropriate.

For larger planning questions, identify the relevant **Community Planning Group** or city department rather than sending every issue into one service-request channel.

## First output

A public before/after receipt:

- problem;
- date reported;
- agency/channel;
- response;
- elapsed time;
- resolution or failure;
- next escalation path.

## Why it advertises the project

“Human Scale” becomes a visible method for making one ordinary trip better.

The receipt is more credible than a slogan.

Official pathways:
- City of San Diego Get It Done: https://www.sandiego.gov/get-it-done
- Community Planning Groups: https://www.sandiego.gov/planning/community-plans/cpg

# Pilot 3 — Bureaucracy Receipt: Can a Normal Person Navigate the City?

## Question

How much human time does one public process require?

Pick one low-risk civic process such as:

- reporting a maintenance problem;
- reserving or finding a community room;
- understanding a planning meeting;
- finding a permit requirement;
- locating a library program;
- finding a park rule;
- requesting a public service;
- learning how to comment on a project.

Record:

- pages visited;
- forms;
- logins;
- duplicate information;
- phone calls;
- transfers;
- hold time;
- jargon;
- elapsed days;
- whether the responsible person was identifiable;
- whether there was a clear appeal/escalation path.

## First output

One **Bureaucracy Receipt** showing where the process was easy and where it became illegible.

Do not only publish complaints.

Document well-designed systems too.

## Why it advertises the project

A clear receipt can be useful to residents and useful feedback to the institution.

The project becomes known for making systems legible rather than merely criticizing government.

# Pilot 4 — Human Scale Library Session

San Diego Public Library is an unusually natural venue for a Human Scale discussion because libraries already function as public knowledge and social infrastructure.

The first session does not need to be a lecture.

Format:

1. 5 minutes — What is Human Scale?
2. 10 minutes — everyone privately takes or discusses the Human Scale Audit;
3. 15 minutes — identify one local friction;
4. 20 minutes — choose one small experiment;
5. 10 minutes — define a receipt and follow-up.

Possible themes:

- human time;
- third places;
- neighborhood shade;
- family/caregiver infrastructure;
- technology that demands less attention;
- accessible public space;
- local food/gardens with real safety planning;
- bureaucracy receipts.

Official library entry point:
- San Diego Public Library: https://www.sandiego.gov/public-library

Before organizing an event, verify current branch/room/program rules directly with the library.

# Distribution Without Spam

## Local online

Share the **result**, not repeated generic promotion.

Good posts look like:

- “We mapped free/low-cost third places along this corridor. What did we miss?”
- “This 0.6-mile walk to transit has almost no shade. Here’s the receipt and city response.”
- “It took 47 minutes and four pages to find the right civic process. Here’s where the information could be simpler.”
- “We tested a quieter phone setup for a week. Here’s what became worse and what improved.”

Bad posts look like:

- “Please check out my philosophy” repeated across unrelated groups;
- mass DMs;
- fabricated grassroots support;
- political fear targeting;
- contacting people repeatedly after no response.

## In person

Useful places to discuss the work can include:

- libraries;
- community planning meetings;
- neighborhood events;
- maker/open-source groups;
- civic-tech groups;
- gardens and restoration projects;
- universities;
- coworking or entrepreneurship communities;
- faith or community organizations where the topic fits.

Do not treat every gathering as a lead list.

Participate first.

## Media / newsletters / podcasts

Pitch a specific question or result:

- “Can a philosophy revise itself when its evidence gets weaker?”
- “We’re measuring the hidden human-time cost of San Diego systems.”
- “A local experiment in public space without another master plan.”
- “Why our ‘return to nature’ chapter got less romantic after the evidence audit.”

The interesting story is the method and the public correction process, not merely the existence of a new doctrine.

# Success Metrics for the First 30 Days

Do not optimize for followers first.

Track:

- Audit completions if analytics can measure the event;
- visits to Start Here;
- people who reach the Lab;
- evidence challenges opened;
- doctrine critiques opened;
- local experiment reports;
- one actual San Diego pilot completed;
- one institution or community group that responds;
- one doctrine change caused by outside input;
- one person other than Thomas who independently explains Human Scale accurately.

A small number of meaningful actions is more valuable than thousands of passive impressions.
