First local laboratory

Human Scale San Diego

Small public experiments that make one piece of ordinary life easier, document the real implementation cost, and create something worth sharing.

Start smaller than a revolution. Make one place easier to live in, document what happened, and learn in public.

The standard

The first San Diego project should be useful even if nobody cares about the philosophy behind it.

Promotion becomes a byproduct of useful work. Instead of repeatedly advertising “Human Scale,” publish maps, receipts, before/after observations, failed experiments and local fixes that residents can actually use or challenge.

Four pilot formats

01 · Community

Third-Place & Human-Time Map

Map free or low-cost places where people can linger: libraries, parks, plazas, recreation, playgrounds, gardens, beaches, markets and other social infrastructure.

Record cost, bathrooms, shade, transit, walking, accessibility, children, opening hours and repeat use.

Output: one neighborhood map + three obvious gaps.

02 · Place

Shade, Walking & Public-Space Receipt

Pick one ordinary route to transit, school, groceries, work, park or daily services. Record shade, crossings, sidewalk barriers, heat, seating, detours and accessibility.

Use the correct official city pathway and publish the response timeline.

03 · Institutions

Bureaucracy Receipt

Measure pages, forms, logins, duplicate fields, calls, transfers, jargon, elapsed days and active human time required for one low-risk civic process.

Document what was well designed too.

Output: one process diagram + friction receipt.

04 · Gathering

Human Scale Library Session

Run a small discussion/workshop: 5 minutes of Human Scale, 10 minutes of Audit, identify one local friction, choose one experiment, define the receipt.

Verify current room/program policies directly with the library before organizing.

How to share without becoming spam

Share a result, not repeated promotion. “We mapped the low-cost third places on this corridor—what did we miss?” is better than “check out my philosophy.”
Bring receipts. Cost, time, agency response, maintenance, participation and failure make the work credible.
Participate before recruiting. Community groups, libraries, gardens, civic meetings, maker groups and local events are communities, not lead databases.
No manufactured grassroots support. No fake accounts, mass DMs, repeated follow-ups after silence, or fear-based political targeting.
Invite critique. Every post can link to the Evidence Atlas or contribution templates, not only a subscribe/follow request.

30-day success test

1 public receipt

One completed San Diego project documented from problem through response or failure.

1 outside correction

One evidence challenge or critique from someone outside the original project that changes the work.

1 second participant

One person other than Thomas who helps run, measure or document an experiment.

1 institutional response

A library, city channel, planning group, organization or other relevant institution engages the concrete issue.

Followers and impressions can be measured later. Early success is useful participation.
