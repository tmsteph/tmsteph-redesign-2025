Energy

Power Without Dependence

Abundant useful energy without hidden pollution, brittle dependency, or allegiance to a fuel identity.

Energy performance is system performance.

What changed

Evidence Audit 06 keeps abundance, low pollution, resilience and technological pluralism while making the chapter more explicitly system-level.

Diagnosis

Abundance ≠ waste

Build enough reliable, affordable, lower-pollution energy for health, comfort, industry, science, computing, mobility, food, water and future capability.

Pollution belongs in cost

Cheap energy is not truly cheap when disease, emissions, cleanup and ecological damage are shifted elsewhere.

Reliability is a portfolio property

Generation, grids, reserves, storage, fuel, demand, maintenance, weatherization, security and operations interact.

Variable renewables require integration

Transmission, storage, flexible demand, geographic diversity and firm resources can integrate variable output in different combinations.

Nuclear is a tool

Low-carbon firm power with real capital, delivery, safety, waste, security and institutional requirements. Evaluate jurisdiction by jurisdiction.

Renewable ≠ impact-free

Land, rivers, habitat, mining, manufacturing, transmission, waste and community consent still matter.

Strong grids can be Human Scale

Network scale can produce diversity, mutual aid, lower reserves, access to resources and lower cost while creating its own vulnerabilities.

Local capability ≠ isolation

Rooftop solar, batteries and microgrids help when they can actually support critical loads through the failure being planned for.

Efficiency is capacity

Reducing waste can free money, infrastructure and generation capacity without requiring scarcity.

Affordability is part of justice

Track who pays, who receives reliability, who bears pollution, who can install local systems and who lives near infrastructure.

Field Guide

Know your critical loads. Medical equipment, refrigeration, communications, lighting, heat/cooling, pumps or mobility can matter differently by household.
Know what backup really does. Verify whether it operates in outages, how long it lasts, what it can power, and how it is maintained.
Reduce waste before buying complexity. Air sealing, insulation, efficient equipment, shade, controls and maintenance can sometimes beat another gadget.
Learn the local system. Utility structure, outage risks, prices, grid constraints, wildfire/storm exposure and resilience programs make dependency more legible.

Program

Build portfolios, not identities

Compare generation, storage, grids, flexible demand, efficiency and emerging technologies across system outcomes.

Expand grids where scale helps

Transmission can unlock diversity and electrification while requiring legitimate siting, environmental review and cost allocation.

Protect critical local functions

Microgrids and distributed resources are especially relevant for hospitals, shelters, water, communications, emergency services and vulnerable households.

Keep nuclear in the comparison

Use it where institutional capacity, financing, delivery and system need justify it; do not use identity as the decision rule.

Protect affordability

Track household burden, industrial costs, reliability, tax/subsidy distribution, stranded assets and workforce effects through transition.

Make external costs visible

Compare standards, pricing, procurement, public investment and incentives by effectiveness, burden, leakage, complexity and durability.

A Human Scale energy test

What human service is this system providing?
Is supply reliable under normal conditions?
Which failures actually matter?
What critical loads must survive?
What is the total system cost and who pays it?
What lifecycle pollution, climate, land, water and material impacts occur?
What institutions and workforce are required?
How quickly can it be built and maintained?
Does local capability add real resilience?
Does interconnection add real resilience?
Can low-income households afford the result?
Can the system expand for future demand?
What evidence would make us change the portfolio?

The direction

Use less where waste adds no value. Build more where energy expands health, comfort, mobility, science, production, resilience and possibility. Keep grids where networks make us stronger and local fallback where it genuinely matters.

The goal is not less power. It is enough clean, reliable, affordable power for human possibility — with the humility to engineer the system rather than worship the technology.
