Purpose
is upstream.

What if the most important question in computing is not what can the machine do? but what are we trying to bring into existence?

Purpose→Feedback→Agency→Creation→New purposes

Before AI, cybernetics. Before cybernetics, a question about purpose.

In the 1940s, researchers including Norbert Wiener, Arturo Rosenblueth, Warren McCulloch and others were wrestling with goal-directed behavior, self-correction, feedback and circular causality in organisms and machines. A 1946 New York Academy of Sciences conference was explicitly titled Teleological Mechanisms.

It would be too simple to say that teleology became cybernetics and cybernetics became AI. The histories branch and overlap. But there is a real intellectual thread: how can a system sense where it is, compare that state with a goal, act, observe the result, and correct itself?

Purpose gives feedback meaning. Feedback turns action into learning.

Not one purpose. The capacity to create purposes.

Classical teleology can sound as if nature is marching toward a single predetermined end. A more interesting possibility is open-ended: living systems develop agency; agents pursue goals; their actions create environments, tools and relationships in which entirely new goals become possible.

Matter → life → agency → purposes → new purposes.

Purpose, in this view, does not need to be a supernatural command imposed on every event. It can emerge wherever systems become capable of maintaining states, selecting actions, learning, coordinating and imagining alternatives.

Computing as an agency amplifier.

Most software starts with capabilities: features, menus, commands, apps. I want to invert that relationship. Start with the human being. What are you trying to understand, change, build, heal, share or become?

Then let the machine organize itself around that intention: help form a vision, turn it into projects, find tools and collaborators, execute reversible work, measure feedback, learn, and return consequential decisions to the human.

Purpose→Vision→Movement→Project→Tools→Community→Open Source

Why open source matters.

A closed tool can satisfy a purpose. An open tool can become material for purposes its creator never imagined. One person's solution becomes another person's primitive. Knowledge compounds. Communities fork ideas, recombine them and send improvements back.

Purpose → creation → sharing → new possibilities → new purposes.

This is the deeper promise I see in open computing: not merely free software, but infrastructure for expanding what people are able to intend and accomplish together.

The direction.

AI should not erase human purpose by replacing people with an optimization target. It should increase our ability to act deliberately. Automation should handle what can safely disappear into the background. Humans should remain the relationship layer and retain accountability where choices matter.

Computers shouldn't merely execute instructions. They should help people discover, pursue and create purposes.

That is the kind of computing I want to build: technology that begins with agency, learns through feedback, grows through community, and leaves people more capable than it found them.
